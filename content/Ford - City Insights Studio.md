During my time at Ford Motor Company in the Research and Advanced Engineering organization, one of the most important programs I led was the **City Insights Studio**. It grew out of a simple but urgent question: if cities are already struggling with congestion, safety, and environmental impact, how do we use science and technology to help them move people more effectively instead of simply adding more cars to the road?

Within Ford's Mobility Research group, we set out to better understand the transportation systems of cities and to give public officials a more tangible way to explore their options. My role was to lead a team of software engineers and user experience experts to design and build an interactive visualization and simulation platform that could turn complex urban mobility data into something people could see, touch, and discuss together.

![[IMG_9595.jpg]]

The City Insights Studio was designed as a physical, collaborative experience for city leaders, planners, and stakeholders. Rather than asking them to interpret static reports or dashboards, we created a system that helped them study their city's transportation network in a shared space. The platform combined advanced traffic simulation tools, large datasets, and live vehicle information from Ford to reveal problem areas across the city, including congestion patterns, crash hot spots, hard-braking zones, and other signals that could guide better planning decisions.

![[IMG_9617.jpg]]

One of the most valuable parts of the system was the way it made mobility data understandable to a wide audience. City officials could gather around the table, explore neighborhoods in context, and have real conversations about the tradeoffs behind streets, parking, safety, and access. We also developed features that provided a live view of parking conditions across the city, helping address one of the most persistent urban frustrations: where to park, and how parking demand influences the overall transportation system.

![[IMG_9614.jpg]]

From a technical standpoint, I am especially proud of what we built. Under the surface, the studio relied on a substantial digital platform with large databases, real-time simulation capability, and data pipelines that brought multiple sources together into a coherent operating picture. For the primary visualization environment, we used **Unity** to render a vivid and interactive urban model. Later, we extended the concept into a web-based experience using OpenStreetMap and also explored an augmented reality version to create additional ways for people to interact with the same mobility data.

![[IMG_9590.jpg]]

The physical installation gave the project its distinctive character. We paired the digital system with 3D-printed buildings placed on top of an interactive display surface, creating a hybrid model that felt both analytical and intuitive. To make that possible, I found a small startup that used photogrammetry to generate the building models, which were then produced in SLA prints. The result was a system that felt less like a presentation and more like a working city-scale instrument.

The studio also supported several modes of interaction. In addition to the main touch-enabled table, users could work from tablets and phones, allowing multiple people to collaborate at the same time. That collaborative layer mattered because transportation problems are rarely solved by a single person or a single department. The studio was most effective when it became a place for planners, researchers, and city stakeholders to work through questions together.

![[mit-city-science.jpg]]

The project was inspired in part by the [City Science group at the MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/groups/city-science/overview/), whose work helped demonstrate how physical interfaces and urban simulation could come together in meaningful ways. We also collaborated with [Professor Hiroshi Ishii](https://www.media.mit.edu/people/ishii/overview/), whose thinking around tangible interfaces strongly influenced how we approached interaction and presence in the studio.

![[IMG_9618.webp]]

Looking back, City Insights Studio was one of the clearest examples of how advanced engineering, design, and research can come together to solve a real public problem. It was not just a visualization tool. It was a way to help cities think more clearly about mobility, safety, parking, and the future of transportation.

![[IMG_9610.jpg]]

For me, the project remains a highlight of my time at Ford because it brought together systems thinking, software, data, physical prototyping, and user experience into a single platform. It showed how mobility research could move beyond theory and become something people could engage with directly.
